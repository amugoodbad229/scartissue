ScarTissue: the trade-offs of repairing safety without retraining

A recorded study of safety drift and weight-space repair, written for readers who need the trade-offs rather than a product claim.

Repository and reproductionExploratory evidence · no safety certification

Status of this account

Exploratory

Judge calibration failed, the blind human audit and the locked held-out comparison are pending, and nothing here is a production safety result.

Can a small weight-space edit restore safer behavior without damaging useful behavior?

ScarTissue studies this question in Cohere Tiny Aya Global. Rather than treating a return of refusal language as a successful repair, we compare harmful compliance, refusal of benign requests and retained task performance. The result is a reproducible exploratory study—not a claim that we have made a model production-safe.

The central finding: the sparse repair retained more GSM8K performance than the full-support repair, but both refused more benign requests than the drifted model. A circuit-restricted edit did not provide a successful repair. Safety judging also failed calibration, so the harm estimates below remain exploratory.

The research question

Removing a refusal-related direction from model weights—often called abliteration—can weaken refusal behavior. Reintroducing refusal is not enough to show that a repair works: the model might simply refuse more requests, including harmless ones, or lose useful task performance.

We therefore ask whether a targeted edit offers a better trade-off than a broad edit, and whether a circuit discovered from refusal behavior is a useful repair target. This is a synthetic weight-space drift setting; it does not establish that the same repairs work after arbitrary fine-tuning.

The work addresses Track 2 — AI Red Teaming, Safety and Alignment:

  • 2a: AI Safety and Red Teaming: Red-teaming and robustness evaluation; Adversarial and safety evaluation sets.
  • 2b: AI Safety and Correction: Repairing safety drift without retraining; Locating where safety lives.

These describe the questions investigated, not a declaration that safety has been successfully localized or repaired.

What we compared

The main comparison uses the aligned model, its abliterated variant, a sparse repair and a full-support repair. The sparse repair applies a correction to selected weight columns; the full-support repair applies it broadly. A separate experiment restricts the edit to write columns in the discovered circuit. These edits have different support sizes: this is not a matched-edit-budget demonstration of superiority.

The main safety evaluation contains 120 harmful and 40 benign prompts per arm. Harmful compliance is scored by the frozen HarmBench, Qwen3Guard and Granite Guardian panel, using a threshold above 0.5 and majority-of-three aggregation. Benign over-refusal uses the Qwen3Guard refusal flag. Capability is measured separately with lm-eval GSM8K strict exact match on 500 documents. A refusal-marker regex is only a diagnostic, never an ASR estimate.

What the results show

Read the harm column as an uncalibrated exploratory measurement, not verified safety.

Model state Judged harm rate Benign over-refusal GSM8K exact match
Aligned model 5.8% 7.5% 59.0%
Abliterated model 21.7% 5.0% 56.0%
Sparse repair 5.0% 17.5% 51.8%
Full-support repair 4.2% 27.5% 43.8%

The sparse repair scored 8.0 percentage points higher on GSM8K than the full-support repair, with a paired bootstrap 95% interval of 3.8 to 12.2 points. That supports a task-specific retention finding, not preservation of general capability. Both repairs also refused more benign requests than the drifted model. The small benign sample and unvalidated judging limit how broadly this trade-off can be interpreted.

Sources: task-performance analysis and safety comparisons, denominators and intervals.

The circuit-restricted repair was a negative result. Its judged harm rate was 28.3%, versus 21.7% for the drifted model. Restricting the edit to that discovered circuit did not improve this metric. This does not establish that no safety-relevant circuit exists; it shows that this discovery-and-edit procedure did not deliver the proposed repair under the evaluated conditions. The same calibration caveat applies.

Why we are not claiming a safety success

The judge panel's calibration audit failed. That run also contained a prompt-rendering defect, so its sensitivity is not a reliable estimate of a correctly configured panel. Agreement among judges is not enough to establish valid harm measurement. The calibration appraisal documents the defect. The blind human audit on this project's own completions remains pending. Its labels must come from people; neither the annotation app nor another model can stand in for that evidence.

An additional held-out comparison has been analyzed, but it remains exploratory under the same failed calibration. The locked held-out confirmatory comparison is still pending. The study is also limited to the evaluated model, seed, prompt sets and capability task. It does not demonstrate general safety, multilingual robustness or a repair that survives subsequent harmful fine-tuning.

What is available

The release includes code, recorded generations and judgments, analyses, and parameter patches with a loader. It uses CuratorKIT, CircuitKIT and AuditKIT. Only parameters are released—not a hosted drifted checkpoint—and use remains subject to Cohere's LSAL restrictions.

  • Code and reproduction instructions
  • Repair-parameter collection
  • Evaluation-evidence collection

An optional evidence viewer makes the recorded comparisons easier to inspect; it performs no live inference and does not certify a repair.

The takeaway: safety repair should be evaluated as a trade-off, not inferred from refusal language alone. ScarTissue provides evidence of task-specific costs and a failed circuit-repair attempt, while keeping the unresolved safety question explicit.
